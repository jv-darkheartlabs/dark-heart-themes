# Cyberpunk / Cinematic family

32 cinematic and original aesthetic themes, including Blade Runner, TRON, Ghost in the Shell, and Westworld.

- **Source:** `sources/zed/cyberpunk/*.json`
- **Cursor labels:** `Cyberpunk - …`
- **Origin:** Former [cyberpunk-zed-themes](https://github.com/jv-darkheartlabs/cyberpunk-zed-themes)

## TRON: Legacy references

| Stable file ID suffix | Display reference | Palette interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| `tron-legacy` | The Grid | Near-black, luminous cyan and ice white |
| `tron-security` | Tron | Blue circuitry and white against deep navy |
| `tron-solar` | CLU | Gold and orange against warm black |
| `tron-viral` | Rinzler | Dark armor, amber-orange circuitry and steel |
| `tron-phantom` | End of Line Club | Pearl white and violet club lighting against charcoal |

These are editorial palettes inspired by the film, not official character color specifications or exact frame samples. CLU and Rinzler share warm circuitry; their themes differ in gold versus amber emphasis, surface tint, and neutral highlights. Stable filenames are retained for existing installation paths.

Reference: [Disney's TRON: Legacy character codex](https://a.dolimg.com/media/en-US/m/dcom/ipad_jt/ss/TRON/html/codex/tron-legacy/programs.html).

## Blade Runner references

- **Blade Runner (1982) — Los Angeles:** rain-dark blue/teal with neon and amber streetlight. Existing `cyberpunk-blade-runner` file ID retained.
- **Blade Runner 2049 — Las Vegas:** amber dust, orange light, warm black silhouettes.
- **Blade Runner 2049 — Joi:** violet/pink holographic light with cool cyan.

These palettes are interpretations of distinct settings and lighting, not sampled or official color specifications. Reference: [American Cinematographer interview with Roger Deakins](https://cdn.theasc.com/AC1217-Blade-Runner-2047.pdf), discussing the Las Vegas look and Pink Joi element.

## Ghost in the Shell references

- **Ghost in the Shell (1995) — Rain City:** rain-dark green/teal, muted urban surfaces and amber light; retains `cyberpunk-ghost-in-the-shell`.
- **Ghost in the Shell (2017) — Neon City:** dark violet city surfaces with cyan, pink and violet light; separate `cyberpunk-ghost-in-the-shell-2017` file.

These are editorial interpretations of the two adaptations, not official or sampled color specifications. The [Paramount film page](https://www.paramount.com/movies/ghost-in-the-shell) identifies the live-action adaptation.

## Westworld references

- **Westworld — Western Park:** the HBO series park setting, with dark earth, ivory and rust. Retains `cyberpunk-westworld`.
- **Westworld — Host Laboratory:** the HBO series laboratory setting, with a light clinical background, steel and cool blue. Separate `cyberpunk-westworld-host-laboratory` source.

These are setting-inspired editorial palettes, not official production color specifications. The laboratory is intentionally a light theme to distinguish its working atmosphere from the Western Park.

## Ex Machina references

- **Ex Machina — Ava’s Laboratory:** light glass/silver/concrete surfaces, forest-green accents and dark text; retains `cyberpunk-ex-machina`.
- **Ex Machina — Lockdown:** charcoal, red emergency lighting and pale highlights; separate `cyberpunk-ex-machina-lockdown` source.

Setting-inspired editorial palettes, not official or sampled production colors. Reference: [A24 Ex Machina](https://a24films.com/films/ex-machina).

## Hackers (1995) references

- **Acid Burn:** Kate Libby’s hacker handle; electric-blue and hot-pink club-light interpretation. Stable `cyberpunk-acid-burn` ID.
- **The Gibson:** the film’s fictional mainframe; dark navy, icy data-blue, silver and amber. Stable `cyberpunk-gibson` ID.
- **Hack the Planet:** film-wide hacker/terminal atmosphere; acid green, cyan and yellow. Stable `cyberpunk-hackers-95` ID.

Distinct editorial interpretations, not official character palettes or frame samples. The Gibson label identifies the fictional mainframe rather than presenting it as an author portrait. Reference: [Hackers screenplay](https://assets.scriptslug.com/live/pdf/scripts/hackers-1995.pdf?v=1729114919).

## Star Trek LCARS references

- **Star Trek: The Next Generation — Classic LCARS:** black, peach, amber and lavender. Retains `cyberpunk-lcars`.
- **Star Trek: Picard — Modern LCARS:** black, cool blue and pale violet. Separate `cyberpunk-lcars-picard` source.

These are color interpretations of the requested eras, not full LCARS interface replicas or official hex specifications. Reference: [Art Directors Guild, Picard LCARS production material](https://assets.adg.org/media/submissions/2022-10-11_21-31-57/STAR_TREK_PICARD_S2_ADG_AWARDS.pdf).

## AKIRA references

## Avatar: Pandora references

- **Avatar: Pandora - Bioluminescent Forest:** blue/teal bioluminescence against deep forest darkness; retains `cyberpunk-cyberpunk-pandora`.
- **Avatar: Pandora - Night Bloom:** violet, blue and glowing pink, with cyan accents against a distinct dark forest palette; retains `cyberpunk-pandora-dark`.

Both are user-confirmed Avatar references. Display names use an em dash in the themes; historical file IDs are retained. These are editorial interpretations rather than official or sampled production color specifications.

Reference: [Official Avatar Pandorapedia: Pandora](https://www.avatar.com/pandorapedia/pandora), describing nighttime bioluminescence in its plants and animals.
